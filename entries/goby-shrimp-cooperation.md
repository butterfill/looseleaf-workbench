# Goby–shrimp cooperation: warning, coordination and partner recognition

What can interspecies cooperation teach us about the mechanisms that sustain coordinated activity? Goby–shrimp associations offer a tractable case: shrimp excavate shelter used by both partners, while gobies provide threat-related information. Three findings are especially worth returning to.

**A warning can precede the signaller's own retreat.** In the Red Sea association studied by \citet{karplus:1979_tactile}, *Cryptocentrus steinitzi* gave rapid tail-flick warnings while *Alpheus purpurilenticularis* maintained antennal contact. Shrimp commonly withdrew while the goby remained outside. Signals were selective with respect to approaching fish, and controlled presentations elicited more warnings to a piscivorous fish than to a similarly sized algal feeder or an empty box. This separates warning a partner from merely providing information through one's own escape. [Evidence and limits](../sources/goby-shrimp-cooperation/karplus1979_tactile.md) · [Publication](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1111/j.1439-0310.1979.tb00286.x).

**Coordination extends beyond alarm episodes.** Field observations of 26 partnerships found that shrimp visibility increased with goby vigilance, and more active gobies had partners that emerged sooner and were more visible \citep{burns:2019_behavioural}. These observational associations leave causal direction and shared environmental influences open. [Evidence and limits](../sources/goby-shrimp-cooperation/burns2019_behavioural.md) · [Publication](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1111/jzo.12673).

**Established partners receive different treatment.** In aquarium choice tests, *Alpheus bellulus* spent more time contacting its familiar *Cryptocentrus cinctus* partner than a size- and colour-matched stranger \citep{mierow:2026_speciesspecific}. This supports partner discrimination in the tested association; the recognition mechanism remains open. [Evidence and limits](../sources/goby-shrimp-cooperation/mierow2026_speciesspecific.md) · [Publication](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1093/beheco/arag117).

For joint-action research, the useful distinction is between **complementary roles**, **ongoing behavioural coordination**, and **partner discrimination**. Whether these amount to joint action under a particular philosophical account remains a further question. The reported behaviours alone establish neither shared intentions nor intentions to inform.

These findings concern different species pairings. They should not be combined into a behavioural profile possessed by every goby–shrimp association.

A question to retain: **What makes a partnership stable when its participants contribute different activities and have different sensory access to danger?**
