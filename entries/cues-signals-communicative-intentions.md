# Cues and signalling in goby–shrimp partnerships: discoveries

Goby–shrimp partnerships combine tactile warnings with information used to choose partners and establish an association. The discoveries show how several forms of information transfer can contribute to cooperation.

**Warnings are selective, contact-dependent and distinct from escape.** In the Red Sea association examined by \citet{karplus:1979_tactile}, gobies gave tail-flick warnings only while the shrimp maintained antennal contact. Controlled presentations elicited more warnings to a piscivorous fish than to a similarly sized algal feeder or an empty box. Shrimp could withdraw while the goby stayed outside. In natural observations, 61% of individual signals and 92% of signal series assessed were followed by shrimp retreat (pp. 179–183). The partner's response therefore depended on more than copying the fish's own flight. [Evidence](../sources/goby-shrimp-cooperation/karplus1979_tactile.md) · [Publication](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1111/j.1439-0310.1979.tb00286.x).

**Different escape patterns accompany different warning rates.** Predator presentations to three goby species revealed that *Cryptocentrus lutheri* withdrew tail-first while danger was farther away and produced relatively few warnings. *Amblyeleotris steinitzi* and *Ctenogobiops maculosus* stayed outside longer, produced more warnings and ultimately withdrew head-first \citep{karplus:2022_tail}. With an approaching model, warning frequency increased near retreat for the latter two species, but showed no comparable distance relationship in the former. This connects signalling to species-specific responses to danger; the proposed explanation in terms of evolutionary lineages remains a hypothesis. [Evidence](../sources/cues-signals-communicative-intentions/karplus2022_tail.md) · [Publication](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1007/s13199-022-00858-9).

**Partner choice can use sight before tactile contact is possible.** In experiments that separated shrimps in watertight transparent compartments, *Myersina macrostoma* preferred its naturally associated shrimp, *Alpheus rapax*, over *A. lobidens*. Preventing water exchange excluded chemical cues in this assay \citep{jaafar:2012_partner}. Visual discrimination can thus contribute to forming a partnership whose later warnings are tactile. The experiment establishes visual attraction, without identifying a specialised display by the shrimp. [Evidence](../sources/cues-signals-communicative-intentions/jaafar2012_partner.md) · [Publication](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1007/s13199-012-0166-2).

**Burrow construction can itself provide information during partnership formation.** In aquarium initiation trials with *Alpheus bellulus* and *Cryptocentrus cinctus*, shrimp digging predicted goby contact \citep{mierow:2026_speciesspecific}. This links a practical contribution—excavating shelter—to another animal's approach. The association supports the authors' interpretation of digging as an initiation cue; it does not establish that digging evolved to advertise shelter or that the shrimp intended to communicate. [Evidence](../sources/goby-shrimp-cooperation/mierow2026_speciesspecific.md) · [Publication](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1093/beheco/arag117).

**Tactile assistance need not imply a blind recipient.** Experiments on *Alpheus heterochaelis* found spatial vision and no significant difference in visual acuity after removal of the protective orbital hoods covering its eyes \citep{kingston:2025_shock}. This challenges a blanket explanation of shrimp sensory limitations by eye coverings. This species is not a goby-associated shrimp, so its measured acuity cannot be transferred to the partnerships above. [Evidence](../sources/cues-signals-communicative-intentions/kingston2025_shock.md) · [Publication](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1242/jeb.250592).

The useful background distinction is between **cues** that recipients can exploit, **signals** adapted to influence recipients, and **intentions to inform**. The discoveries establish information use and warning functions much more directly than evolutionary origins or communicative intentions.
