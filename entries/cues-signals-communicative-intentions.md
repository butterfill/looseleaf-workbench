# Cues, signals and communicative intentions: separate questions

What additional evidence is needed as we move from informative behaviour to signalling, and then to intentional communication? This is a provisional working distinction for comparing cooperative interactions.

| Question | What needs establishing |
|---|---|
| **Cue:** Does a feature or behaviour provide usable information to another animal, irrespective of whether it evolved for that purpose? | Evidence that recipients detect and use it. |
| **Signal:** Is the feature or behaviour adapted to influence recipients through their responses? | Evidence about communicative function and evolutionary history, beyond an informative correlation. |
| **Communicative intention:** Does the producer intend to inform the recipient? | Evidence bearing on the producer's cognitive capacities and intentions, interpreted against an explicit account of intentional communication. |

These questions concern different explanatory levels. A biologically functional signal need not be intentionally produced to inform, and evidence that a recipient exploits information need not establish a specialised signal. Definitions of intentional communication also vary; intending an effect on another's behaviour and intending to inform should be distinguished.

**The goby–shrimp case gives the distinction empirical traction.** \citet{karplus:1979_tactile} reports contact-dependent tail-flick warnings, selective responses to approaching fish and shrimp withdrawal while the goby remains outside. This supports a tactile warning function beyond merely observing the goby's escape. Those observations do not by themselves reconstruct the evolutionary origin of the behaviour or establish a communicative intention. [Checked source note](../sources/goby-shrimp-cooperation/karplus1979_tactile.md) · [Publication](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1111/j.1439-0310.1979.tb00286.x).

A promising question: **When does coordination depend on a specialised signal, and when can partners coordinate by exploiting information already available in each other's ordinary activities?** Answering this would help distinguish mechanisms of cooperation without inferring cognitive sophistication from behavioural complexity alone.
