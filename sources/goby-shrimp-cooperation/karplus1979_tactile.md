# Tactile warnings in a Red Sea goby–shrimp association

\citet{karplus:1979_tactile}. *The Tactile Communication between Cryptocentrus steinitzi (Pisces, Gobiidae) and Alpheus purpurilenticularis (Crustacea, Alpheidae).* Zeitschrift für Tierpsychologie, 49, 173–196. [Publication](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1111/j.1439-0310.1979.tb00286.x).

## Evidence checked

Sections III–IV distinguish antennal contact, warning movements and retreat. In 35 hours of natural observations of 20 associations, goby retreats were rare (12), whereas warnings were much more frequent. Warnings were recorded only during antennal contact. Shrimp retreated following 61% of the 147 individual signals assessed and 92% of 78 signal series (pp. 179–181). These are different response units, not independent replication samples.

Section V compares presentation of a piscivorous fish, a similarly sized algal feeder and an empty transparent box, with 10 associations per condition and each association examined once (pp. 182–183). The predator elicited more warning signals. The methods also measured shrimp exits because the availability of antennal contact constrains warning production.

## What this establishes and leaves open

The association supports threat-selective tactile warnings and recipient withdrawal beyond simply copying the goby's own retreat. Warning responses are probabilistic and context-sensitive. The observations do not establish a represented shared goal, an intention to inform, or the evolutionary history needed to demonstrate every element of an adaptation-based signal definition. Generalisation beyond this species pairing requires comparative evidence.
