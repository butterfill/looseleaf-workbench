# Partnership formation and familiar-partner discrimination

\citet{mierow:2026_speciesspecific}. *Species-specific behaviors drive the initiation and maintenance of snapping shrimp-goby mutualisms.* Behavioral Ecology, 2026, arag117. [Publication](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1093/beheco/arag117).

## Evidence checked

The experiments used aquarium-housed *Alpheus bellulus* and *Cryptocentrus cinctus* purchased from retailers. Initiation trials examined contact, following and digging with differing substrate availability (Materials and methods, p. 2; Results, p. 3). Gobies contacted and followed shrimp more frequently; shrimp digging predicted goby contact. The latter association does not independently establish digging as an evolved communication signal.

In the partner-preference assay (p. 2), a shrimp was placed between its established goby partner and a size- and colour-matched stranger. Contact duration was recorded for five minutes. The analysis included shrimp identity as a random effect. Shrimp spent significantly more time contacting their established partner (Results, p. 3; Fig. 4b and Table 6).

The separate partner-seeking assay found shrimp made first contact in 10 of 15 trials, but the difference from equal likelihood was not significant (p. 3; Fig. 4a). This does not establish that shrimp alone maintain partnerships.

## Interpretive limits

Familiar-partner preference supports discrimination under the test conditions. It leaves the sensory recognition mechanism open and does not establish a human-like representation of individual identity, shared commitment or an intention to communicate willingness. Aquarium results concern this pairing, not all goby–shrimp associations. Partner preference and causal necessity for long-term maintenance are distinct claims.

The source is valuable for separating partnership formation, partner discrimination and moment-to-moment warning behaviour.
