# Behavioural interdependence in the field

\citet{burns:2019_behavioural}. *Behavioural interdependence in a shrimp-goby mutualism.* Journal of Zoology, 308, 274–279. [Publication](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1111/jzo.12673).

## Design and findings

The authors filmed 26 *Ctenogobiops feroculus*–*Alpheus djeddensis* partnerships at One Tree Island, generally for 30 minutes on three consecutive days (Materials and methods, pp. 275–276). Goby activity counted locomotory movements. “Vigilance” meant positioning at the burrow entrance within tactile range; it was an operational measure, not a direct measure of attention or warning signals.

Results (p. 276, Table 1 and adjoining text) report greater shrimp visibility with goby vigilance, and earlier shrimp emergence and greater visibility with goby activity. Gobies emerged before shrimp in all recorded trials. Goby activity was repeatable across the observation days; vigilance allocations were not.

## Interpretive limits

This is field evidence of behavioural association. It does not experimentally isolate a causal influence of either partner or rule out common environmental influences. “Vigilance” should not be treated as a direct assay of threat detection, and increased visibility should not be equated with measured fitness gain. The Discussion offers sensory and cooperative explanations that remain hypotheses.

The case is useful for distinguishing ongoing behavioural coupling from discrete warning exchanges. It does not test shared intentions.
