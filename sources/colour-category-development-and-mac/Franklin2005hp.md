# Color term knowledge does not affect categorical perception of color in toddlers

**Citation:** \citet{Franklin:2005hp} — Anna Franklin, Ally Clifford, Emma Williamson and Ian Davies (2005), *Journal of Experimental Child Psychology*, 90(2), 114–141.

**Library identity.** The @Research text is searchable as papers:franklin:2005_color and reports normalized key franklin:2005_color. The **verified BibTeX entry** retrieved separately identifies this work as **Franklin:2005hp**. Use that exact key, not the normalized search alias.

**Design and findings.** Experiment 1 assessed English children aged about two to four years with differing colour-term knowledge. Experiment 2 investigated toddlers in Himba, whose colour lexicon differs from English. Experiment 3 examined whether the effect reflected categorization under different task conditions. Overall categorical responding occurred regardless of mastery of the relevant labels, and did not rise in magnitude with naming proficiency. Himba toddlers without acquired colour terms also showed category-sensitive performance across tested distinctions.

**Cautions.** The findings concern specified stimuli, tasks and childhood age ranges; absence of a word-knowledge effect on overall *magnitude* does not preclude language changing processing strategies, lateralization or later category representations. It should not be presented as a blanket proof that linguistic experience never affects colour-related performance.

**Use here.** Blocks the simple developmental explanation in which colour labels first create categorical responding, while permitting later lexical calibration.
