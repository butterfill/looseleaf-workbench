# The development of color perception and cognition

**Citation:** \citet{maule:2023_development} — John Maule, Alice E. Skelton and Anna Franklin (2023), *Annual Review of Psychology*, 74, 87–111.

**Kind of source.** An interdisciplinary **review**, not an independent new data collection. It is the most useful checked high-level source for separating sensory colour discrimination, prelinguistic categorization, lexical mapping, linguistic input and behavioural consequences of learning.

**Relevant synthesis.** Section 4.1.1 evaluates infant category findings from habituation, recognition memory and visual search, including why stimulus-equating critiques matter; it interprets the Skelton infant mapping as consistent with biologically grounded categories while warning against direct identity with adult lexicons. Section 4.1.2 reviews slow lexical mapping, early comprehension, input frequency and boundary refinement; it stresses that learning a central use and converging on an extension are different problems. It also notes methodological limitations and inconsistent adult replication of split-field lateralization claims. Other parts of the review place colour categorization alongside colour constancy, salience and general task factors.

**Limit.** The review's references to earlier experiments are **secondary evidence** unless those primary works have independently been accessed. Its synthesis predates the 2025 German/Japanese and Japanese slow-mapping papers checked separately.

**Use here.** Supports the architecture of the main note and helps weight conflicting findings, without substituting for direct checks where available.
