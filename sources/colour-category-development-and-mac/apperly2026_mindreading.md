# Mindreading as asynchronous coordination: The MAC account

**Citation:** \citet{apperly:2026_mindreading} — Ian A. Apperly, Rory T. Devine and Stephen A. Butterfill (2026), *Cognition*, 274, 106569. DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2026.106569.

**Question and central argument.** Competence in mental-state concepts does not by itself explain how an individual generates a particular plausible attribution or why people with similar conceptual competence differ in practical mindreading. Real-world evidence underdetermines particular mental-state ascriptions and targets can be only boundedly rational. Success therefore requires more than identifying *any* logically possible mental state: an ascription should be recognizable as plausible and appropriate by relevant others.

**MAC proposal.** A mindreader generates a **restricted set of candidates** and then selects among them using background norms, assumptions and expectations aligned through social interaction. Past interactions and anticipation of later evaluations enable those standards to operate in the absence of a present partner: **asynchronous coordination**. The theory makes predictions about persistent individual differences and the development, flexibility and social specificity of mindreading.

**Status and limits.** The paper is a theoretical synthesis and proposal with engagement with empirical mindreading literature. It does not itself establish that interaction rather than passive statistical exposure is necessary for acquiring each standard. It also does not test colour perception or lexical category acquisition. A parallel from colour is an **independent analogy**, not a claim of empirical confirmation made by the authors.

**Use here.** Provides the target explanation: socially aligned standards for generating and selecting plausible attributions, which may be used alone and differ across groups.
