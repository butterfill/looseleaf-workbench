# Lateralization of categorical perception of color changes with color term acquisition

**Citation:** \citet{franklin:2008_lateralization} — Anna Franklin and colleagues (2008), *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 105, 18221–18225. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.0809952105.

**Design.** Toddlers were grouped by production/comprehension of the relevant blue–green terms (“learners” versus “namers”) and searched for a colour target against within- or between-category backgrounds, appearing in left or right visual fields. Eye-movement initiation latency was the outcome.

**Result.** The overall categorical-search advantage was not materially greater among children with better term knowledge. Its **visual-field lateralization** differed: learners primarily showed the advantage in the left visual field (right hemisphere), and namers in the right visual field (left hemisphere). Thus lexical development coincided with a change in where an effect was expressed, without an increased overall effect.

**Limits.** Naming groups were not created through experimental language training and differed in age. A visual-field effect is an indirect and method-dependent proxy for hemispheric processing. Split-field laterality effects have proved fragile across work in the area; the study by itself does not establish a reliable neural “handover” to language or a causal effect of labels.

**Use here.** A possible example of reorganization of **deployment** rather than creation of category sensitivity; comparatively low weight in the overall argument.
