# Cross-linguistic comparison of color word learning in German and Japanese children

**Citation:** \citet{saji:2025_crosslinguistic} — Noburo Saji, Henrik Saalbach, Gerlind Große, Cornelia Schulze and Mutsumi Imai (2025), *Scientific Reports*. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-23286-3.

**Design.** German adult naming data (including 26 adult participants and a dense set of swatches) were used to characterize lexical frequency, category size and adult naming inconsistency. German three- and five-year-olds were tested on naming centroids and sets of colours; earlier Japanese data were reanalysed for comparison. The distinction is between **centroid mapping** (correct term for a best example) and **boundary delineation** (fit of a child's extension to adult usage, assessed by an f-measure).

**German model results.** Table 4 shows a robust input-frequency contribution to centroid mapping; category size is **not significant** there (reported p = .251). Table 5 shows poorer adult-like boundary delineation for terms with more adult naming inconsistency (p = .0157), and significant contributions from frequency (p = .02137) and category size (p = .00021). The Japanese results likewise identify adult naming consistency as informative for boundary learning, with language-specific differences in other predictors.

**Reporting issue.** One sentence in General Discussion states that both input frequency and category size predicted German centroid success, contradicting Table 4. Follow the reported model and do **not** turn the paper's conclusion into a clean exclusive separation of frequency-versus-consistency mechanisms.

**Limits.** Relatively small, cross-sectional samples and production measures; input and adult lexical structure are predictors, not experimentally randomized causes. Cross-language modelling uses previously collected Japanese data.

**MAC relevance.** The learned **extensions** of categories reflect how consistently the community classifies borderline cases; this connects naturally to social alignment without proving reciprocal coordination.
