# Slow mapping in color word acquisition across languages: Evidence from Japanese children

**Citation:** \citet{yang:2025_slow} — Jiale Yang, Karen Dobkins, Katie Wagner, Yuiko Sakuta, So Kanazawa and Masami K. Yamaguchi (2025), *Frontiers in Developmental Psychology*, 3:1641593. DOI: 10.3389/fdpys.2025.1641593.

**Design.** A Japanese follow-up of \citet{wagner:2013_slow}, with 29 analysed children aged 2.5–3.8 years. Two production tasks and one comprehension task tested colour terms including the distinct Japanese label *mizuiro* (light/sky blue). The comprehension task always followed production.

**Results.** Incorrect production labels repeated on 47% of relevant paired trials against a 17% chance estimate. The authors classify 78% of production errors as overextensions. Production errors were more often perceptually proximal than expected (66% vs 31%); comprehension errors showed a smaller above-chance proximal bias (36% vs 27%, reported p = .037). These outcomes broadly corroborate systematic early meanings and gradual lexical boundary refinement across languages.

**Limitations and checks.** Small sample and no a-priori power analysis; fixed task order; weak sensitivity of some secondary analyses (post-hoc power for comprehension proximity given as .42). The prose in Results apparently calls correct responses for *red* “69%, 10 out of 29”, an arithmetically inconsistent count/percentage. Avoid reusing that value until reconciled. Production proximity is substantially stronger than the comprehension result, and pragmatic approximations remain one possible contributor to production errors. Venue selectivity should prompt especially careful scrutiny of design and statistics, without treating the findings as invalid solely due to venue.

**Use here.** Cross-language corroboration of slow mapping, with weight proportional to the small and methodologically constrained replication.
