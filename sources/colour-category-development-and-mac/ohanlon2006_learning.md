# Learning in context: Linguistic and attentional constraints on children's color term learning

**Citation:** \citet{ohanlon:2006_learning} — Catherine G. O'Hanlon and Debi Roberson (2006), *Journal of Experimental Child Psychology*. DOI: 10.1016/j.jecp.2005.11.007.

**Why especially important.** This is the closest experimental evidence in the checked literature for **feedback contingent on the learner's own classification**. The outcome is learning colour-word meanings, not directly a measure of category perception or interpersonal norm enforcement.

**Studies.** Experiment 1 assigned 60 English-speaking three-year-olds to five training groups (corrective, semantic or referential contrast, and two controls). Under corrective contrast, after a child chose a sample and supplied a known term, the response identified the target term while explicitly contrasting it with **the child's own term**. Corrective training produced faster and more extensive learning than control and semantic-contrast conditions; not every direct comparison with referential contrast was significant. Experiment 2 replicated a corrective advantage with Italian-speaking children and showed effects of a distinct lexicon for blue/green-adjacent colours. Experiment 3 enhanced colour's task salience without the same corrective linguistic feedback, also improving learning substantially.

**Causal interpretation.** Training manipulated feedback conditions, so it supports the **efficacy** of response-contingent correction in these tasks. Because corrective feedback also highlights the relevant perceptual dimension, and salient non-corrective tasks can be similarly effective, it does **not** isolate the unique efficacy of interaction-based coordination. Experiment 3 and the corrective condition are in different cohorts/experiments, not an equivalence trial of identical inputs.

**MAC relevance.** Generates a concrete hypothesis: do corrections responsive to the learner's choices produce an enduring, socially aligned boundary beyond what perceptually and statistically matched evidence supplies?
