# Constraints on children's color term acquisition

**Citation:** \citet{franklin:2006_constraints} — Anna Franklin (2006), *Journal of Experimental Child Psychology*. DOI: 10.1016/j.jecp.2006.02.003.

**Kind of source.** This is a reflective commentary on competing accounts, especially conceptual abstraction, attention and linguistic learning; it is **not itself a new randomized colour-learning experiment**.

**Central contribution.** The question “Why are colour terms difficult?” requires specifying a learning stage and an appropriate comparison domain. A child may be able to perceive and match colours, have some notion that colour terms form a class, and still be unable to apply individual terms accurately. Conceptual, attentional and linguistic constraints can therefore contribute at different stages, and methods used to isolate them should be tested with converging evidence. The apparently decreasing developmental age of mastery also complicates global claims that colour terms are uniquely late or uniquely difficult.

**Use here.** Disciplines the analogy: prelinguistic categorization, early partial lexical knowledge, boundary learning and changes in task performance should be evaluated as **separate explanatory targets**, rather than compressed into one “social construction of colour” event.

**Limitation.** A conceptual and methodological synthesis cannot establish the magnitude or relative causal contribution of those constraints by itself.
