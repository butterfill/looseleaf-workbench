# Colour perception changes with basic colour word comprehension

**Citation:** \citet{forbes:2023_colour} — Samuel H. Forbes and Kim Plunkett (2023).

**Design.** Two eye-tracking experiments with English-learning twelve- and nineteen-month-old children, relating responses near an adult blue–green boundary to parent-reported earliest comprehension of *blue* or *green*. Experiment 1 analysed 63 children; 62 had sufficient colour-word inventory information (15 marked as understanding a relevant term, 47 not). Experiment 2 analysed 58 children and included pupil responses to repeated colour alternation.

**Results.** In Experiment 1 there was an interaction between reported term knowledge and trial condition on gaze time course, most visibly in one stimulus condition; there was no term-knowledge main effect. In Experiment 2, total looking-time analyses provided no colour-word-knowledge effect, but pupil dynamics differed between within- and between-adult-category alternation as a function of reported word comprehension.

**Central caveats.** The authors explicitly say that equating hue steps in **Munsell units rather than JNDs** means these experiments **do not test categorical perception**. Parent-reported comprehension was observational; known/unknown groups were correlated with age, which was not included jointly in the key first-experiment knowledge model. The gaze findings were condition-specific and the study acknowledges possible preference, habituation, socioeconomic and sample restrictions. Pupil size reflects arousal/attention as well as perceptual processing.

**Responsible inference.** Early colour-term comprehension **correlates with changed boundary-near gaze and pupil responses**. The experiments do not show that learning labels causally reshapes categorical discrimination or low-level vision.

**Use here.** A potentially important early bridge between words and performance, but low-to-moderate evidential weight for a strong Whorfian claim.
