# \citet{frank:2016_perceptualcognitive}: training, action knowledge and performance dissociate

**Reference.** Frank, Cornelia, William M. Land & Thomas Schack (2016), “Perceptual-Cognitive Changes During Motor Learning: The Influence of Mental and Physical Practice on Mental Representation, Gaze Behavior, and Performance of a Complex Action,” *Frontiers in Psychology* 6, 1981. DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2015.01981. Exact Personal Papers metadata key: `frank:2016_perceptualcognitive`.

**Method.** 45 putting novices in three groups over three days: physical practice only, combined physical and mental practice, or no practice. Measures included putting accuracy, “quiet-eye” gaze and perceptual–cognitive organizational structure of putting-related BACs, before and after acquisition and at retention. Training was not designed to change the segmentation *boundaries* of putts.

**Results.** Both practising groups improved performance (Abstract; Results, Outcome Performance). More elaborate mental representational structures and longer quiet-eye duration were found especially after **combined physical and mental practice**, not to a comparable extent with physical practice alone (Results, Mental Representation Structure; Discussion). The authors conclude that long-term action-related knowledge and execution performance are linked but need not change identically across different training regimes.

**Relevance.** Challenges any easy inference that better motor ability by itself changes what counts as an action in *thought*. It also motivates including a mental-imagery and conceptual-learning condition in the proposed chunking experiment.

**Limit.** The measure is long-term **perceptual–cognitive associations**, not independently demonstrated sensorimotor representational units or observer judgements about what temporal portion is an action. Imagery may recruit the motor system as well as visual and declarative processes.
