# \citet{schack:2006_representation}: basic action concepts in skilled motor performance

**Reference.** Schack, Thomas & Franz Mechsner (2006), “Representation of motor skills in human long-term memory,” *Neuroscience Letters* 391(3):77–81. DOI 10.1016/j.neulet.2005.10.009. Key copied from Personal Papers \`citation-key\`: \`schack:2006_representation\`.

**Procedure/result.** Players varying in tennis expertise were examined using structure dimensional analysis of mental representation (SDA-M) of **basic action concepts** (BACs) for the tennis serve. Experts' concepts grouped into functional/biomechanical clusters approximating preparation, strike and follow-through phases; less experienced players and nonplayers had less functionally coherent clustering and more between-person variability. The expert factor-analysis table and accompanying individual clustering discussion specify the arrangement, with expert group \`n=11\` (results, around original journal p.80). The authors take these structured long-term memory representations to function as perceptual–cognitive references for controlling skilled movement.

**Why this matters.** Provides a concrete alternative to arbitrarily splitting time: meaningful units can be linked to functional submovements and their roles in achieving outcomes. It is a candidate method to measure a privileged motor-relevant structure *independently* of observing a particular action clip.

**Crucial limitation.** BACs were elicited as **cognitive association structures in long-term memory**, not independently measured online motor plans. Neither neural motor chunks nor action-individuation judgements were demonstrated. Comparing experts with novices conflates motor skill, conceptual expertise and shared verbal descriptions of serve phases. A test must establish that the relevant representational *format and causal function* is motoric, not just action-related.
