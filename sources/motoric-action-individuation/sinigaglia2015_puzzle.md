# \citet{sinigaglia:2015_puzzle}: the interface between the motoric and thought

Sinigaglia, Corrado & Stephen A. Butterfill (2015), “On a puzzle about relations between thought, experience and the motoric,” *Synthese* 192(6), 1923–1936. DOI: 10.1007/s11229-015-0672-x. Verified Personal Papers key: `sinigaglia:2015_puzzle`.

**Access:** Original Personal Papers full text, abstract and §§1–7, particularly the conclusion. The paper's cited empirical results were not all independently reverified.

**Proposal.** Motor representations involved in action performance may also occur in observing action and influence which goals observers judge an action directed towards. Because these representations are inferentially isolated from explicit thinking, the puzzle is how their content can systematically influence thought. The proposed route is via motor-shaped experiences which provide reasons for action judgements.

**Why relevant.** Its conclusion goes beyond improved recognition to suggest that capacities to think about some *types of action* might depend on motor representational capacities. An object-index analogy raises the possibility that motor organization contributes to tracking *this action* as an individual through its unfolding.

**What is unresolved.** The cited findings concern motor contributions to outcomes, experience and recognition, not an independently manipulated repertoire of **action individuals**. Experience mediation is a hypothesis, not already evidence that arbitrary movement portions can or cannot be thought of as actions.
