# \citet{pomp:2024_action}: object contact and object knowledge in segmentation

**Reference.** Pomp, Jennifer, Annika Garlichs, Tomas Kulvicius, Minija Tamosiunaite, Moritz F. Wurm, Anoushiravan Zahedi, Florentin Wörgötter & Ricarda I. Schubotz (2024), “Action Segmentation in the Brain: The Role of Object–Action Associations,” *Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience* 36(9):1784–1806. DOI 10.1162/jocn_a_02210. Exact Personal Papers metadata key: \`pomp:2024_action\`.

**Design.** Manipulation videos with ordinary objects and with shaped pieces of dough allowed authors to compare stronger and weaker learned object–action associations, while extracting contact/non-contact transitions among hands, objects and surroundings. Participants watched during fMRI and later marked subevent boundaries; MRI and behavioural analyses contrasted the object study with a separate **dough study**, rather than fully randomized conditions in one controlled sample.

**Finding.** Touching and untouching events were strong, reliable anchors for subjective segmentation. **The dough condition showed *more systematic* boundary behaviour in some measures**, despite weaker associations, rather than the simplistic prediction that meaningful objects always produce stronger segmentation. Object information nevertheless affected processing of event transitions and some neural contrasts, notably at untouching (Results, Behavioural Reliability and fMRI; Discussion).

**Consequence for the conjecture.** Perceived relational changes and object-specific knowledge offer substantive alternative explanations for where observers place boundaries. Premotor/parietal activity at those times does not show that motor representations *establish* units of thought.

**Limitations.** Between-study comparison can confound samples or stimuli; objects vs dough also differ in familiarity, affordance, complexity and visual structure. The original paper itself lists these limitations. Neither direct motor perturbation nor judgments of which stretch is *one action* were measured.
