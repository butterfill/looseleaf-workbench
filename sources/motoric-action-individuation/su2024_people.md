# \citet{su:2024_people}: changes of action and goal are distinguishable

**Reference.** Su, Xing & Khena M. Swallow (2024), “People can reliably detect action changes and goal changes during naturalistic perception,” *Memory & Cognition* 52(5):1093–1111. DOI 10.3758/s13421-024-01525-8. Exact Personal Papers metadata key: `su:2024_people`.

**Method.** Untrained participants viewed recordings of everyday actions from first- and third-person perspectives, marking **action changes**, **goal changes**, or **event boundaries** under different instructions. Analyses compared temporal agreement, relationships among kinds of marks, pixel-based visual change, and object touch onsets/offsets. Much of the analysis of task order used only first tasks to reduce carryover (Method; Results; Discussion).

**Results.** Respondents converged on action-change and goal-change locations. Action changes were reported much more frequently (~18.1 presses/min) than goal changes (~3.94 presses/min). The relationship between these judgements and event boundaries survived controls for a global visual-change measure and touch onset/offset; the two types of changes are not simply reducible to those specific physical events.

**Contribution.** The paper experimentally separates fine-grained **action change** from higher-order **goal change**, supporting the idea that something more sophisticated than instantaneous movement information matters for segmentation. It offers outcome measures closer to the target of structured action thought than general mirror activation.

**Limitations.** Instructions provide explicit examples of what participants should call an action versus a goal; familiarization and task repetition might shape responses. More detailed perceptual predictors may remain. It includes **no intervention on motor representation**, and a reported “change of action” may still be different from individuation of one coherent extended action.
