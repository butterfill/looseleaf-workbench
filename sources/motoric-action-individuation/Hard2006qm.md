# \citet{Hard:2006qm}: RETRACTED — do not rely on reported experimental results

**Publication status — critical.** Hard, Bridgette Martin, Sandra C. Lozano & Barbara Tversky (2006), “Hierarchical Encoding of Behavior: Translating Perception Into Action,” *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 135(4):588–608, DOI 10.1037/0096-3445.135.4.588, **was formally retracted in 2008**. Its authors wrote that **Hard and Tversky believed the results could not be relied upon**, with Lozano accepting responsibility for the need to retract. Verified original published retraction notice: Hard, B. M., Lozano, S. C., & Tversky, B. (2008), “Retraction of Hard, Lozano, and Tversky (2006),” *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 137(4):672, DOI [10.1037/a0013753](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1037/a0013753); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18999359/). **Do not cite the retracted results as established evidence of any psychological effect.** The original paper's legacy key \`Hard:2006qm\` is indeed verified by an \`@article\` entry in Personal Papers, but **key correctness does not establish evidential validity**.

## Historical content, not findings to rely on

The 2006 paper proposed that observers segment novel object-assembly sequences into coarse and fine action units and that hierarchical encoding facilitates later performance. It reported an **enclosure** index that allegedly predicted subsequent assembly better than simple boundary-alignment and several experiments involving segmentation instructions, verbal descriptions and later performance. The main workbench entry mentions it **only to identify the proposed measurement distinction as a historically described possibility and, especially, its retracted status**. Its numerical outcomes, associations and causal interpretations must be treated as withdrawn, *not* offered in support of the motoric hypothesis or its competitors.

The distinction between a boundary that aligns across grains and genuinely nested structure can be motivated independently by part–whole conceptual analysis and other nonretracted event-perception research. Any empirical use of the specific **enclosure** measure needs **independent validation from unretracted work**.

## What to do next

Verify whether independently conducted, nonretracted research replicates the particular link from hierarchical **encoding while observing** to **hierarchical execution**. Investigate test–retest reliability and direct action-identity tasks without relying on the 2006 statistical claims. A related Lozano–Hard–Tversky paper, *Perspective taking promotes action understanding and learning* (2006), also has a 2008 retraction notice (DOI 10.1037/a0013754). Do not substitute that work as confirmation.
