# \citet{stadler:2021_shared}: shared boundary positions among taekwondo experts

**Reference.** Stadler, Waltraud, Veit S. Kraft, Roee Be’er, Joachim Hermsdörfer & Masami Ishihara (2021), “Shared Representations in Athletes: Segmenting Action Sequences From Taekwondo Reveals Implicit Agreement,” *Frontiers in Psychology* 12, 733896. DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2021.733896. Exact key `stadler:2021_shared` is provided by Personal Papers bibliographic metadata (not inferred).

**Method.** Twenty-four taekwondo experts and 29 physically trained control participants indicated natural boundaries within videos of taekwondo forms, including more and less familiar styles. They received no instructions prescribing a segmentation grain. Behavioural agreement within subjects and between subjects was examined along with self-reported segmentation strategies. Not all experts specialized in the same form/style (Materials and Methods, Participants).

**Key contrast.** The individual **chosen grain size was highly variable**, and **expertise did not systematically affect the average temporal separation of boundaries** (time between responses, `Z=-1.19,p=.23`; Results, Response Frequency). Yet experts had greater **within-group overlap of boundary *locations*** than novices (Results, Within-Group Agreement and Discussion). This effect is particularly relevant to the *identity of preferred segments*, not just the number of perceived parts. It extended to relatively unfamiliar forms.

**Uncertainty.** Skilled performers may share internal motor organizations, but they also share a vocabulary for techniques and training conventions, awareness of functional movement units, semantic representations and judgements of good form. None was independently varied; the study cannot distinguish a motorically supplied unit from a conceptually learnt partition. The absence of object-directed outcomes means the relationship to reaching/grasping needs further work.
