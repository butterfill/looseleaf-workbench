# \citet{bonini:2011_grasping}: motor acts nested in larger goals

Bonini, Luca et al. (2011), “Grasping Neurons of Monkey Parietal and Premotor Cortices Encode Action Goals at Distinct Levels of Abstraction during Complex Action Sequences,” *Journal of Neuroscience* 31(15), 5876–5886. DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5186-10.2011. Verified Personal Papers key: `bonini:2011_grasping`.

**Access:** Original Personal Papers full text: abstract, task, Results, Discussion and Conclusions. Figures were available via text captions, not separately verified at image level.

**Experiment/findings.** Two macaques executed complex sequences containing two grasp acts with final eating or placing goals. Recordings included 122 inferior-parietal PFG and 89 premotor F5 neurons. Some grasp-related responses were insensitive to the wider goal; others showed late goal preference, and a subgroup in PFG showed early selectivity depending on goal information held before execution. The authors interpret this as coding of action outcomes at several levels of abstraction.

**Why relevant.** Motor organization represents both component acts and the goals of a whole sequence; it is plausible that such units could scaffold a corresponding organization of thought about observed actions.

**Unestablished connection.** Neural coding in performing monkeys is not yet evidence that human thoughts individuate actions on these boundaries, or that motor chunking *causes* observed segmentation. That step requires cross-domain and preferably intervention-based evidence.
