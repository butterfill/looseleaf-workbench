# \citet{zacks:2004_using}: movement features and goal knowledge

Zacks, Jeffrey M. (2004), “Using movement and intentions to understand simple events,” *Cognitive Science*. DOI: 10.1016/j.cogsci.2004.06.003. Verified Personal Papers key: \`zacks:2004_using\`.

**Access:** Original Personal Papers full text; checked abstract, introduction, design and results/discussion portions. Not all statistical models were independently recalculated.

**Findings.** Movement features of simple animations predict participants' temporal event boundaries. The relation varies with the intended actions represented and grain of segmentation, suggesting an interaction between physical discontinuity and top-down knowledge of intentional structure.

**Contribution and limit.** This provides a serious alternative to motoric causation. Motion/acceleration *in a stimulus* is not a **motor representation in the observer**. Nor do button-pressed segmentation points by themselves establish which temporal portions count as individual actions *in thought*. Future experiments should control for these sources of boundary selection.
