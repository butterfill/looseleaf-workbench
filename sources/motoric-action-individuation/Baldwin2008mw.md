# \citet{Baldwin:2008mw}: statistical learning can produce action segmentation

Baldwin, Dare, Annika Andersson, Jenny R. Saffran & Meredith Meyer (2008), “Segmenting dynamic human action via statistical structure,” *Cognition* 106(3), 1382–1407. Verified Personal Papers key: `Baldwin:2008mw` (case sensitive).

**Access:** Original full text in Personal Papers, abstract, stimulus and control descriptions, Experiment 4 and general discussion.

**Findings.** Participants detect transitional-statistical regularities in artificial sequences of human actions. The authors tried to eliminate many ordinary goal/intentional and low-level discontinuity cues, and demonstrated learning despite constraints on verbal recoding. This makes statistical sequence learning a plausible generator of *segmentation*.

**Relevance and limitation.** A partition of the motion stream might arise from general sequence learning even without a motoric ontology of actions. But statistically extracted segments in this paradigm are not automatically actions that one can track, classify and reason about as *individual actions*. Neither exclusively motoric nor exclusively statistical accounts follow.
