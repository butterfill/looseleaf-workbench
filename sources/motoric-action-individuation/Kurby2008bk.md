# \citet{Kurby:2008bk}: event segmentation, prediction and memory

**Reference.** Kurby, Christopher A. & Jeffrey M. Zacks (2008), “Segmentation in the perception and memory of events,” *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 12(2):72–79. Exact legacy BibTeX key verified: \`Kurby:2008bk\`.

**Content.** This review describes event perception as a continuous updating process: observers maintain event models to support prediction, updating them around changes that temporarily raise prediction error. It reviews behavioural evidence for consistent perceptual boundaries; multiple grain sizes and part–whole hierarchies; involvement of posterior temporal, parietal and frontal regions at boundaries; and consequences for working and long-term memory. The “Events and their boundaries,” “Hierarchical event perception,” “Memory for events,” and “How does segmentation help?” sections are particularly relevant.

**Use and limitations.** The review makes it plausible that action-related thought has a temporally organised substrate and explains why changes in prediction or goals can mark units. It is **not** itself a test of motor representation individuating an action. Event models and event boundaries can be defined in perceptual or conceptual terms. Its predictive account offers a serious, potentially non-motor alternative to motorically privileged units.
