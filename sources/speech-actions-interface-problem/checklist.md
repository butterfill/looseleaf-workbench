# Source checklist — speech actions and the intention–motor interface

[Main entry](../../entries/speech-actions-interface-problem.md) · [Companion entry on gestures as objects of speech perception](../../entries/phonetic-gestures-and-speech-perception.md)

**Scope of this checklist.** The present inquiry concerns how intentions connect with motor representations in *producing* speech, and whether perception and learning of speech actions contribute to the action concepts involved in that connection. The companion [speech-perception checklist](../phonetic-gestures-and-speech-perception/checklist.md) now covers the motor theory, categorical perception, articulatory gestures as proposed perceptual objects, and their general evidence base. Those sources are intentionally not duplicated here unless they bear directly on the interface argument.

The research is **selective and exploratory**, not a systematic review. Original papers were examined to different extents during the investigation. A verified key or an available full-text view alone is not evidence of a complete critical reading. No new publication-specific assessment note is created in this editorial revision without the required separate full-text source audit.

## Open questions and outstanding checks

- [ ] Choose one concrete speech-action case—perhaps an intended word with a studied consonantal or vowel contrast—and map the contents and formats of **intention, lexical choice, phonological plan, task goal and motor representation**. Distinguish which causal transitions are modelled and which are assumed.
- [ ] Reconstruct DIVA/GODIVA and FACTS from the original model papers as well as the reviews; evaluate whether their apparent intention–motor bridge is instead a narrower phonological–motor bridge.
- [ ] Examine the latest literature on preparation, cancelled speech and auditory feedback; test which designs discriminate planned action, overt movement, and sensory prediction while separately manipulating explicit speech intentions.
- [ ] Survey motor versus perceptual training on novel phonetic contrasts; look for independent measures of **event-based perception, action concepts, and flexible intentional control**, rather than category judgments and imitations alone.
- [ ] Identify plausible testable differences between deference, executable-schema links, learned translation, conceptual-space planning, dimensional biasing and experience-mediated acquisition. An arbitrary training benefit is insufficient.
- [ ] Check the older manuscript's developmental references concerning early phonetic discrimination and later explicit phonetic-action thought. Do not assume the two measures establish a causal order between experience and action concepts.
- [ ] Audit the original journal texts, methods and counterevidence for every substantive research claim; add publication-specific assessment notes only once full-text checks and precise locators are complete.
- [x] Resolve the seven previously author–date works in Personal Papers and update the main entry (11 October 2026).
- [ ] Investigate boundary cases such as unfamiliar accents, speech imitation, silent articulation, sign-language articulation and movements that preserve an acoustic target by different motor means, to assess what generalises.
- [ ] Treat possible dialogues/joint-speech extensions as subsequent projects, not as proof of an interface for collective goals.
