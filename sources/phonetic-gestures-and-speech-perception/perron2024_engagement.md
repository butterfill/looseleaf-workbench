# \citet{perron:2024_engagement} — which production networks overlap with effortful listening

**Source:** Perron, Vuong, Grassi, Imran and Alain (2024), *Engagement of the speech motor system in challenging speech perception: Activation likelihood estimation meta-analyses*, *Human Brain Mapping* 45. DOI: [10.1002/hbm.70023](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1002/hbm.70023). Research ref: papers:perron:2024_engagement.

## Meta-analytic evidence

Coordinate-based activation likelihood estimation (ALE) was applied to 89 speech-perception studies (26 speech in noise, 19 spectrally degraded, 45 linguistically complex; one study contributed to two conditions) and 35 speech-production studies. Searches were updated 2 May 2024 (*Methods—Speech perception meta-analysis*, *Results*, Table 1).

Speech-perception/production activation overlap was strongest around left frontal operculum (FOC) and planum temporale (PT), with differing context-dependent involvement of pre-SMA and posterior/lateral inferior frontal cortex. In *Discussion §4.3* the authors **explicitly examine the absence of primary motor and premotor overlap** in their pooled ALE findings; one should not describe the outcome generically as motor cortex everywhere responding more under noise.

## Why this matters

An important correction to a simplistic prediction that degraded speech invariably increases one articulator-specific premotor signal. There are multiple functional and anatomical subregions, and speech-in-noise, spectral alteration and linguistic complexity are not equivalent demands. Compare \citet{du:2014_noise} and \citet{perron:2024_left} without equating their ROIs or temporal measures.

ALE identifies spatial convergence of published peaks, **not** causality, shared computations, neuronal populations or representational format. Reporting thresholds, heterogeneous contrasts, missing-coordinate studies and non-imaging sensorimotor effects affect coverage. Additional executive-control and task factors remain plausible.
