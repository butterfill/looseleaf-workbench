# \citet{gick:2009_aerotactile} — airflow contributes to voiced/aspirated judgments

**Source:** Gick and Derrick (2009), *Aero-tactile integration in speech perception*, *Nature* 462. DOI: [10.1038/nature08572](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1038/nature08572). Research ref: papers:gick:2009_aerotactile.

## Method and positive finding

Naïve listeners heard aspirated and unaspirated consonant–vowel syllables (/pa, ba, ta, da/) while brief, minimally noticeable and inaudible air puffs were timed to the syllables and directed to neck or hand. The speech-puff pairing increased reports of aspirated syllables: congruent puffs supported 'pa/ta', incongruent puffs could shift perception of 'ba/da' (figures 1–2; *Methods Summary*). Effects at hand and neck argue against requiring familiar tactile contact at the lips.

This is a real multimodal cue integration effect relevant to the **public articulatory-event** thesis. But tactile information is about a possible airflow consequence, not necessarily the speaker's performed gesture or private motor intention; a Bayesian causal-inference or decision account is an alternative.

## Critical context

The study employed restricted **closed-choice syllable classification**. The published follow-up \citet{derrick:2019_aerotactile} ([assessment](derrick2019_aerotactile.md)) found airflow-congruence effects in closed-choice but not open-choice syllable identification; its two experiments nevertheless differed in participant groups, stimuli and procedures, so it does not isolate response format alone. It must not be presented as unconditional improvement in natural speech comprehension.
