# \citet{Browman:1992da} — gesture units in Articulatory Phonology

**Source:** Catherine P. Browman and Louis Goldstein (1992), *Articulatory Phonology: An Overview*, *Phonetica* 49, 155–180. Verified Research ref: papers:Browman:1992da.

## What is claimed

Articulatory Phonology treats **gestures as basic phonological contrast units and temporally extended articulatory events**. They are formalised as *dynamical vocal-tract tasks*: forming or releasing relevant constrictions rather than merely recording a fixed set of muscle activations. An utterance is a coordinated constellation of gestures which may overlap in time. This gives the theory a route to coarticulation and fluent-speech variation without always deriving the outputs from deletions or substitutions of independent, timeless segments.

The account reaches beyond production kinematics: it proposes an approach to phonological contrast, allophony, connected speech, speech errors and developmental acquisition. It is a productive theoretical framework, rather than an empirical proof that *listeners must perceive intended motor commands*.

## Relevance and limitations

The most important distinction for the main inquiry is between **a unit of production/phonological organisation** and **the object of auditory perception**. Gesture-based formalisation explains why sound and segment structure need not correspond one-to-one. It does not independently establish how the listener represents the speaker's articulation, whether the listener simulates it, or whether the *perceived object* is a gesture.

The related direct-realist treatment of phonetic events as public actions should be checked against \citet{Goldstein:2003bn}. This must not be conflated with the classical motor theory's appeal to intended commands.
