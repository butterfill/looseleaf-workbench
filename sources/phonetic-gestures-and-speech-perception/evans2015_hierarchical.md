# \citet{evans:2015_hierarchical} — abstract syllable identity in somatomotor cortex

**Source:** Evans and Davis (2015), *Hierarchical Organization of Auditory and Motor Representations in Speech Perception: Evidence from Searchlight Similarity Analysis*, *Cerebral Cortex* 25. DOI: [10.1093/cercor/bhv136](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1093/cercor/bhv136). Research ref: papers:evans:2015_hierarchical.

## Test and results

This is a stronger positive representational test than an activation contrast. Seventeen participants entered the final fMRI analysis. Listeners heard syllables in clear, noise-vocoded and sine-wave forms from different speakers, after training for degraded stimuli, and made one-back repetition judgments; they also completed a separate production localiser (*Method*, pp. 4774–77). Searchlight representational similarity analysis (RSA) contrasted models for *surface acoustic form* and *syllable identity*.

Primary auditory cortex preferentially tracked acoustic form; lateral temporal cortex reflected both dimensions; an area overlapping left primary motor and somatosensory cortex carried information about syllable identity generalising across pronounced acoustic differences but comparatively little surface-form information (Fig. 4 and *Results*, pp. 4778–84). Production recruited some of the same somatomotor territory. Adjacent premotor activation increased for degraded speech.

## What it supports—and what it leaves undecided

Evidence that somatomotor regions can encode abstract **heard-syllable identity**, not merely coactivate during listening. Yet identity abstracted from acoustics does not uniquely equal a representation *in articulatory/motor format*: a learned auditory category or sensorimotor mapping could generate identity-based neural geometry. The authors expressly state in *The Role of Somatomotor Representations in Speech Perception* that fMRI activity does not establish a causal role.

The findings complement—but do not trivially refute—\citet{cheung:2016_auditory}, whose intracranial study compared listening and production patterns for phonetic-feature organisation rather than the same RSA models.

## Limits

An attention-demanding one-back task, prior training with unusual speech transformations, modest sample and fMRI spatial/temporal limitations. A genuine acoustic-invariant gesture representation requires a model or intervention that separates syllable identity, acoustics, articulatory information and task decisions.
