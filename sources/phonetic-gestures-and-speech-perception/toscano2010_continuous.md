# \citet{toscano:2010_continuous} — early continuous encoding, later category effects

**Source:** Toscano, McMurray, Dennhardt and Luck (2010), *Continuous Perception and Graded Categorization: Electrophysiological Evidence for a Linear Relationship Between the Acoustic Signal and Perceptual Encoding of Speech*, *Psychological Science* 21(10), 1532–1540. DOI: [10.1177/0956797610384142](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1177/0956797610384142). Research ref: papers:toscano:2010_continuous.

## Test and results

The experiment used acoustically manipulated speech varying in **voice-onset time (VOT)** with an auditory oddball task and event-related potentials. The question was whether the phonetic category boundary warps auditory encoding itself or whether early encoding tracks acoustic cues relatively continuously.

The authors report that early **N1 amplitude** varies with VOT in a largely one-to-one fashion, even while listeners classify stimuli phonologically; later **P3 amplitude** is also sensitive to fine-grained variation but is influenced by phonological category. The crucial contrast is between graded early auditory coding and later category-sensitive processing, not a denial that phonetic categories affect behaviour.

## What this bears on

The results undercut a simple version of categorical perception where auditory representation is immediately and completely recoded into discrete units. This is a strong experimental complement to \citet{mcmurray:2022_myth}'s methodological review. It does not identify *all* phonetic representations, nor isolate articulatory gestures: VOT, ERP components and the specific task offer a selective window into speech perception.
