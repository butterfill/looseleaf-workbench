# \citet{baek:2026_dynamic} — parallel acoustic and categorical signals

**Source:** Baek, Kim, Maeß, Grigutsch and Sammler (2026), *Dynamic acoustic-to-categorical representations of phonemes and prosody along ventral and dorsal speech streams*, *Nature Communications* 17. DOI: [10.1038/s41467-026-75240-0](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1038/s41467-026-75240-0). Research ref: papers:baek:2026_dynamic.

## Experiment and principal finding

Twenty-nine German-speaking participants entered the analyses. The study morphed the real-word pair *Bar/Paar* along word-initial voice-onset time (/b/–/p/) and, independently, statement/question pitch contour, sampling five levels of each dimension per participant from larger continua. During MEG they made alternating two-choice phoneme and prosody identifications. The authors fit individual sigmoid response functions and then used time-resolved representational similarity analysis (RSA), separating modelled *acoustic morph step* and *behaviourally defined category* dissimilarities; multivariate transfer entropy assessed information flow (*Results*, Figs 1–3; *Methods*).

The main result is substantial temporal/spatial **overlap of acoustic and category-related neural geometry**, rather than clean serial acoustic-then-category stages. For phonemes, strong representational evidence lay in bilateral temporal ventral-stream regions, especially posterior temporal cortex for categorical components; prosody had more extensive frontal/dorsal involvement (*Results—From acoustic to categorical representations*; *Discussion*).

## Interpretation and conceptual caveat

Helpful corrective to reading \citet{toscano:2010_continuous} as showing an exclusively early-continuous/later-categorical cascade. Crucially, authors infer 'categorical perception' from better sigmoid than linear identification fits. Under \citet{mcmurray:2022_myth}, such a decision boundary does **not** independently establish elimination of within-category auditory detail. Even RSA's 'category model' derives from the participant's fitted judgments, and morph level is only an approximation to complete acoustics. Compare acoustic-feature controls in \citet{daube:2019_simple}.

## What it cannot establish

No intervention showing gestures are necessary; no direct test of intended vs realised gestures. Two word tokens, single phoneme contrast, explicit tasks and model correlations constrain generality to natural continuous speech.
