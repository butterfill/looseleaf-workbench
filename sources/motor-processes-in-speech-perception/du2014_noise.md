# \citet{du:2014_noise} — phoneme patterns in noise across auditory and motor systems

**Source:** Du, Buchsbaum, Grady and Alain (2014), *Noise differentially impacts phoneme representations in the auditory and speech motor systems*, *PNAS* 111. DOI: [10.1073/pnas.1318738111](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1073/pnas.1318738111). Research ref: papers:du:2014_noise.

## Data and claim

Participants identified four natural /ba/, /ma/, /da/, /ta/ syllables across six noise conditions. fMRI univariate and multivariate analysis examined how phoneme decoding in ventral premotor/Broca regions and auditory cortex varied with signal-to-noise ratio (SNR; *Results*, pp. 7126ff; *Materials and Methods*).

Better multivoxel discriminability in left ventral premotor/Broca regions persisted at intermediate adverse SNRs (at or above −6 dB), whereas the tested auditory ROI patterns were reliably discriminative principally under very low noise. Increased speech-motor BOLD response tracked *worse* behavioural accuracy with noise, a possible compensatory pattern. The paper interprets this as robust motor-system phonemic coding under challenging input.

## Cautions for interpretation

This is correlation and model decoding, not motor intervention; an explicit four-choice categorisation task can recruit decision, working memory and attention processes. Acoustic feature structure was not experimentally dissociated from gesture identity; multivariate decodability cannot by itself specify 'motor' content. Contrast the later MEG noise results in \citet{perron:2024_left} and the broader region-wise meta-analysis \citet{perron:2024_engagement}. Differences in brain regions, analysis and stimulus regimes can explain apparent conflicts without implying a single all-purpose noise prediction.
