# \citet{rogalsky:2011_are} — preserved receptive speech after mirror-system lesions

**Source:** Rogalsky, Love, Driscoll, Anderson and Hickok (2011), *Are mirror neurons the basis of speech perception? Evidence from five cases with damage to the purported human mirror system*, *Neurocase* 17. DOI: [10.1080/13554794.2010.509318](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1080/13554794.2010.509318). Research ref: papers:rogalsky:2011_are.

## Lesion test and outcomes

Five patients with lesions selected by location (involving Broca's region, inferior parietal lobule, or both) were tested on spoken-word/picture comprehension and word/nonword discrimination, with varied phonemic and semantic foils; lesion mapping enabled assessment of temporal extension (*Methods*, pp. 180–82).

Across the sample, performance was strong (mean word comprehension 97%; word discrimination A-prime approximately .94; nonword A-prime approximately .90). Individuals with frontoparietal damage generally performed at or near ceiling; more pronounced impairment tracked encroachment on temporal auditory regions (*Results*, pp. 182–84). Thus damage in purported mirror regions need not cause a corresponding loss of basic speech perception.

## Relevance to the entry

An additional patient-based challenge to **necessity** of a speech-production/mirror circuit for receptive phonetic processing; goes beyond a single case in \citet{stasenko:2015_causal}. It is logically consistent with motor modulation, compensation and different contributions to explicit phoneme manipulation. Lesion dissociation cannot decide whether a recovered auditory percept happens to represent a publicly realised gesture.

## Limits

Five heterogeneous lesions and chronic injury, potential reorganisation, clinical selection, restricted tasks and high performance ceilings. Some tests involve overt pointing or judgments and may conceal finer impairments; no selective ablation of a gesture representation. The reported relation between temporal damage and receptive deficits is suggestive, not an experimental randomised intervention.
