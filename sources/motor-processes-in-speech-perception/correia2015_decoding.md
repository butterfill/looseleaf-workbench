# \citet{correia:2015_decoding} — cross-generalised articulatory features

**Source:** Correia, Jansma and Bonte (2015), *Decoding Articulatory Features from fMRI Responses in Dorsal Speech Regions*, *Journal of Neuroscience* 35. DOI: [10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0977-15.2015](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0977-15.2015). Research ref: papers:correia:2015_decoding.

## Design and principal result

Ten Dutch-speaking participants heard consonant–vowel syllables while attending to them but without an explicit production or phoneme-identification task. The main runs presented 72 sounds with substantial inter-trial gaps. Single-trial fMRI searchlight classifiers were trained to distinguish articulatory *place* for one manner (e.g. stops) and tested on another (e.g. fricatives), with analogous cross-generalisation tests for manner and voicing (*Materials and Methods*, pp. 15016–20).

Significant generalisation for place and manner appeared in bilateral temporal, sensorimotor and some pre-/postcentral and inferior frontal regions; voicing effects had a partly distinct distribution (*Results*, Figs 4–5; Table 2, pp. 15021–23). The across-manner transfer is more informative than decoding an individual speech token from an acoustically correlated motor response.

## Interpretation for the entry

A positive argument that listening-related patterns contain information structured by **articulatory feature distinctions** without overt speaking. It supports mixed or sensorimotor representations; it does not uniquely show recovery of the speaker's *intended commands* or that gestures constitute perceptual objects. Acoustic/coarticulatory regularities can generalise across stimuli too; successful phonetic-feature decoding is not itself a proof of code format.

This is a deliberate contrast with \citet{arsenault:2016_no} (a negative passive-listening somatotopy replication) and \citet{cheung:2016_auditory} (auditory organisation in vSMC). Their comparisons are nonidentical: cross-generalisation across manner, motor-lip topography, and cross-task feature geometry are different predictions.

## Limits and pending checks

Small sample; fMRI rather than causal perturbation; passive-with-attention rather than natural comprehension. The article acknowledges possible vowel coarticulation cues and notes that subvocal rehearsal was not objectively monitored. Further acoustic-control modelling and comparison against non-speech controls are needed before attributing the decoded structure specifically to gestures.
