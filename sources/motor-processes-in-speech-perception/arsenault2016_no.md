# \citet{arsenault:2016_no} — failed passive-listening somatotopy replication

**Source:** Arsenault and Buchsbaum (2016; online 2015), *No evidence of somatotopic place of articulation feature mapping in motor cortex during passive speech perception*, *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* 23. DOI: [10.3758/s13423-015-0988-z](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.3758/s13423-015-0988-z). Research ref: papers:arsenault:2016_no.

## Question, replication design and result

The authors attempted to reproduce the articulator-specific motor map reported by \citet{pulvermuller:2006_motor} during **passive listening**. Thirteen right-handed English-speaking adults completed fMRI sessions for speech perception (eight syllables balanced for place, manner and voicing) and a silent-articulation/miming localiser, with speech perception first. Analyses tested the previously reported coordinates, functional motor ROIs, anatomical ROIs and multivariate classification (*Methods*).

The localiser identified reliable lip-versus-tongue production patterns, classifiable up to 92% on single trials. Neither univariate speech-evoked responses nor multivariate classifiers showed the predicted place-of-articulation somatotopy during passive listening; Bayesian tests provided evidence favouring the null in the tested comparisons (*Results*, tables of Bayesian evidence; *Discussion*).

## What the failure bears on

Directly undermines a **general, obligatory somatotopic mapping in motor cortex during passive listening**, not every motor contribution. Compare the positive pattern generalisation in \citet{correia:2015_decoding} and the context-dependent effects in \citet{du:2014_noise}. The rival studies operationalise different features and listening states; reconciliation needs matched stimuli, tasks, ROIs, power and acoustic confound controls.

## Qualifications

Thirteen participants and particular phoneme/task choices constrain null generality. The authors consider differences from the original motor localiser and scanning protocols. Absence of passive somatotopy does not refute motor effects in noisy discrimination or voluntary categorisation; and it says little directly about the perceptual *object*.
