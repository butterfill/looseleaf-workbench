## Study and primary result (UNPUBLISHED PREPRINT)

In this **unpublished bioRxiv preprint**, 42 adults were initially recruited, 32 proceeded to imaging and 24 French-speaking participants remained for the reported analyses following exclusions (*Methods—Participants*). Native French and non-native Mandarin CV syllables were heard under clear and fixed noisy conditions and compared with lip/tongue movement localisers. An fMRI classifier trained on **production-related lip vs tongue movements** generalised above chance to **perception of native /p/ and /t/ in noise in right precentral cortex** (reported group mean 60% vs 50% chance; *Results*, Fig. 2). The transfer was not significant for the tested fricative controls; theoretical feature-based RSA suggested wider bilateral motor–auditory encoding of phonetic similarities (Figs 3–4).

The unpublished result addresses *production/perception shared articulatory features* more directly than a generic listening-activation contrast. It may qualify the absence of passive-listening maps in \citet{arsenault:2016_no}, although the task, noise, laterality, classifier training and sample differ.

## Substantial reasons for caution (UNPUBLISHED PREPRINT)

The key statistically detectable cross-modal transfer was **restricted to degraded native plosives and one hemisphere**, not an unrestricted property of all speech. Imaging fMRI patterns do not prove that motor activation is causally necessary or that the listener experiences the *speaker's intended gestures*. A single bilingual voice, artificial triplets, fixed noise, an articulatory localiser trained on different movements, participant exclusions and feature/acoustic covariance limit broader claims. No independent replication or peer review was available.

Treat this **unpublished bioRxiv result** as a hypothesis-generating, priority-follow-up item, not as part of the established evidential basis. It is a different work from the published review \citet{tseng:2026_embodied}.
