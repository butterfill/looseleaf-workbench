# Orbital hoods and spatial vision

\citet{kingston:2025_shock}. *Shock wave-damping orbital hoods cover the eyes of snapping shrimp (Alpheus heterochaelis) without impairing spatial vision.* Journal of Experimental Biology, 228, jeb250592. [Publication](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1242/jeb.250592).

## Evidence

The authors combined microCT reconstructions and optical estimates with optomotor behavioural trials comparing animals with intact orbital hoods and animals whose hoods had been removed.

Results and Discussion, “Morphology indicates orbital hoods make insignificant contributions to vision” (Fig. 2), estimate hood focal lengths at 33±7 mm, much longer than the approximately 71±9 μm distance between hood and underlying eye. This predicts little effect on spatial focusing.

“Behavioral trials show orbital hoods neither help nor hinder spatial vision” (Fig. 3; pp. 4–5) reports no significant hood-status effect in global or pairwise models. Animals followed moving stripe stimuli at several widths, demonstrating spatial vision. The study estimates a minimum resolvable angle of 4 degrees from these trials.

## Interpretation and limits

The intervention and optical modelling support the conclusion that these eye coverings do not substantially impair spatial acuity in this species under the test conditions. A nonsignificant difference is not proof of exact equality, and acuity is only one component of vision; the Discussion distinguishes possible effects on light sensitivity.

*Alpheus heterochaelis* is not a goby-associated shrimp. Its acuity cannot be assigned to the shrimp in the communication studies. The result is relevant to evaluating blanket claims that orbital hoods render snapping shrimp blind, not to directly measuring the sensory advantage of gobies in a particular partnership.
