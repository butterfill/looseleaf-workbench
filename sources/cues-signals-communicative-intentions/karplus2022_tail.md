# Species differences in warning rate and retreat

\citet{karplus:2022_tail}. *The tail flick warning signals and the molecular phylogeny and evolution of gobiid fishes associated with burrowing alpheid shrimps.* Symbiosis, 87, 121–128. [Publication](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1007/s13199-022-00858-9).

## Evidence

Methods §§2.1–2.3 compare three field associations: *Cryptocentrus lutheri*–*Alpheus djiboutensis*, *Amblyeleotris steinitzi*–*A. purpurilenticularis*, and *Ctenogobiops maculosus*–*A. rapax*. Treatments involved an approaching predator model and a live predator restrained in a transparent box. Each species comparison used 10 replicates; one reported restrained-predator comparison has nine observations for *A. steinitzi*. The approaching-model data for *A. steinitzi* had already been published and are reused in this comparative article (§2.2).

Results §3.1 report average model distances at retreat of approximately 58 cm for *C. lutheri*, 15 cm for *A. steinitzi* and 20 cm for *C. maculosus*. The former retreated tail-first in nine of ten trials; the latter species invariably retreated head-first. Their mean warning counts per model trial were 3.8, 21.1 and 15.0 respectively. Warning rate varied with distance from the retreat point in the latter two species but not in *C. lutheri* (Fig. 2, pp. 124–125).

The restrained-predator comparison also found fewer warnings from *C. lutheri* (§3.2, Fig. 3). Recipient shrimp showed no significant between-species difference in retreat versus non-retreat following warning series (Table 1, p. 126).

## Interpretation and limits

The evidence establishes different signalling and withdrawal patterns in the three tested associations. The Discussion connects low warning rate to earlier withdrawal and high rate to remaining outside as danger approaches. Its extension to two evolutionary clades is explicitly a signalling-rate hypothesis. Three species, paired with different shrimp species, do not establish a universal lineage effect. Nonsignificant recipient differences do not demonstrate equivalent protective benefits or fitness.
