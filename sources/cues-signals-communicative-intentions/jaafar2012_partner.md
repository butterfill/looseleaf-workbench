# Visual partner choice before contact

\citet{jaafar:2012_partner}. *Partner choice in Gobiid fish Myersina macrostoma living in association with the alpheid shrimp Alpheus rapax.* Symbiosis, 56, 121–127. [Publication](https://libkey.io/libraries/3907/10.1007/s13199-012-0166-2).

## Evidence

Experiment II used 30 *Myersina macrostoma* in a shrimp-choice tank. Shrimps occupied transparent, watertight end compartments preventing water exchange, designed to exclude chemical cues. After acclimatisation, goby position was recorded after five minutes; attraction was scored by proximity to a shrimp compartment (Methods §2.4, p. 123).

The gobies were attracted to *Alpheus rapax* against an empty compartment, showed no significant attraction to *A. lobidens* against an empty compartment, and preferred *A. rapax* when both species were presented (Results §3.4, Table 2, pp. 124–125). The shrimps were similar in size. Other experiments found attraction to burrows and larger burrows, but no significant preference between the two shrimp sizes tested (§§3.2–3.5).

## Interpretation and limits

The compartment design supports a role for visual cues in initial attraction to a suitable partner species. The result concerns visual attraction in a short aquarium test, not proof of successful long-term partnership formation or identification of a particular visual feature. It does not establish that the shrimp produced a specialised display, or that either animal intended to inform the other.

This gives a concrete distinction between information used to choose partners before contact and the tactile warning exchanges documented within other established partnerships.
